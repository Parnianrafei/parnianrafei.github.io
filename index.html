I study how imagining the future shapes the habits we form and break, and how that knowledge can improve treatment for addiction.

I'm a PhD researcher in psychology at Trinity College Dublin, supervised by Prof Claire Gillan. My thesis, Making and Breaking Habits, combines large-scale online behavioural experiments and computational approaches to understand when behaviour is goal-directed and when it becomes habitual.

Before Trinity, I trained in clinical psychology at the University of Tehran, where my MSc used fMRI to study episodic future thinking in cannabis users. I've worked across neuroimaging, cognitive interventions and international addiction-medicine consortia, and have co-authored more than twenty papers and preprints, including in Trends in Cognitive Sciences and JAMA Psychiatry.

I'm currently seeking postdoctoral positions and fellowship collaborations in cognitive neuroscience, computational psychiatry and addiction research. Get in touch.

Research interests

Habits, compulsivity and goal-directed control

How the balance between flexible, goal-directed choice and automatic habit shifts in everyday behaviour and in addiction, studied with outcome-devaluation and response-remapping paradigms.

Future-oriented cognition and behaviour change

Episodic future thinking and prospective memory, and whether simulating the future helps people override unwanted habits.

Substance use, interventions and treatment prediction

The neurocognitive effects of substance use and psychoactive compounds, novel cognitive interventions targeting reward-based decision-making, and predicting treatment response with neuroimaging and natural language processing.

Current projects

  • Does time pressure reveal habits?

    Comparing outcome-devaluation and response-remapping paradigms to test whether limiting response time exposes habitual control in humans.

    With S. Suddell, E. Buabang, G. Urcelay and C. Gillan. In press, Cognitive, Affective, & Behavioral Neuroscience.

  • Goal simulation and habit control

    Testing whether thinking about goals disrupts habitual responding, using a behavioural outcome-devaluation paradigm.

    With C. Gillan. Presented at Trinity College Neuroscience Research Day, 2026.

  • Language and temporal focus in quitting smoking

    Using natural language processing to identify linguistic markers of temporal focus in participants of a smoking-cessation trial of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy.

    With J. Mildner, J. Bricker and C. Gillan. Presented at APS 2026, Barcelona.

  • Neural dynamics of covert habit activation

    Tracking how habits are activated and controlled in the human brain, even when they don't show up in behaviour.

    Led by E. Buabang, with C. Gillan and colleagues. Preprint on bioRxiv.

  • Episodic foresight for craving in opioid use

    Testing cue-induced retrieval and reconsolidation with episodic foresight (CIREF), a framework I developed, in people receiving methadone maintenance treatment.

    With M. Toulami, K. Ghasemi, H. Ekhtiari and T. Rezapour. Presented at Lisbon Addictions 2025.
